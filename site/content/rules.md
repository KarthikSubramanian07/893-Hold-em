# The rules of 893 Hold'em

893 Hold'em is a cooperative poker game for 2 to 6 players. The crew plays together against one masked House. You can see the cards of the two players beside you but never your own, and nobody may talk about cards: the only signal is a nod.

## The rules in four lines

1. **You see the hands of the two players beside you, never your own.** The House shows one card and plays its best five with the board.
2. **After the turn, each player may nod at one of the two they can see**: "you're good". One nod each per hand, or pass.
3. **After the river, everyone picks In or Out at once.**
4. **Every hand that's In plays the House on its own.** Each one that wins: one step toward the door. Each one that loses: one step toward the sirens. A tie moves nothing for that hand. Out: nothing moves, but the clock runs.

Reach the door and you get away clean. Reach the sirens and the bar is raided. After ten hands it is last orders, and the night ends at dawn.

## A hand

1. **The deal.** The crew sits in a ring. Each player gets two hole cards, facing out: you see the cards of the two players beside you, not your own. At two or three players that is everyone else; from four up, nobody sees the whole table. Spectators see no hole cards. The House gets two cards; the first is face up.
2. **The nod (頷).** The flop and the turn come out: four board cards. Each player may nod at one of the two players they can see, or pass. Nods stay secret until everyone has nodded, then they all turn over together.
3. **The call.** The river comes out, so all five board cards are showing. Everyone picks at once:
   - **In (入)**: your hand plays the House. It moves the marker one step, toward the door if it wins and toward the sirens if it loses.
   - **Out (出)**: you sit this one out. Your hand stays off the table.
4. **The showdown.** Every card flips, including your own, for the first time. Each hand that's In plays the House's best five on its own, and the table shows which won and which lost.

If the timer runs out, you pass on the nod and sit out the call.

## The track

One marker sits between the sirens and the door. It starts at zero. How far each end is depends on the table and on how many are in the crew, and the tables were tuned by simulating thousands of nights. The rules drawer and the lobby show the numbers for your table before you start.

## Reading the table

A nod says "of the two hands I can see, this one looks good." Everyone gets one nod and has two neighbours, so if both look good they have to pick. That makes a pass, or a nod that went past you to your neighbour's other side, information too. Both of your neighbours nodding at you is a good reason to go In. No nods is a reason to think twice, though the nodders can't see the river, and they can't see their own hands either. Every In hand plays alone: going In with a weak hand costs the crew a step, and sitting out a winner costs it the step it would have gained.

## Tables

The House holds two cards at every table. What changes is the track.

- **Shitamachi (下町)**, the corner bar. Forgiving, with the sirens a long way off. Built so a crew that plays well gets away clean about 70% of the time.
- **Kabukichō (歌舞伎町)**, the neon. The sirens are closer. About 50%.
- **Oyabun (親分)**, the boss's room. The sirens are close and the door is far. About 30%.
